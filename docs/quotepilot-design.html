The intentional boundaries
No CRM integration, e-signature, lead scraping, recordings, multi-currency, automatic email or legal drafting in the first release.
Price and timeline are user-controlled. A model cannot change approved rates. Missing facts remain questions. Changing a commitment invalidates approval.
Compare with the buyer's existing ChatGPT + template workflow. If this does not save meaningful time, stop before building SaaS.

04 / Protect the capital
Release money only against evidence.
GATE 1 / MAX S$5,000
Prove people will pay
Interviews, sample, business/legal review and manual delivery. Thirty qualified sales conversations with fewer than three paid pilots → stop coding and revise the offer.
GATE 2 / MAX S$10,000
Prove repeat usefulness
Release only after five paid pilots, three repeat uses, median ≥50% preparation time saved and no unsupported commitments in final packs.
GATE 3 / MAX S$15,000
Prove renewals and costs
Ten monthly subscribers; at least eight renew once. Measured cash CAC ≤S$200 and contribution payback ≤3 months before increasing growth spend.
S$170,000 ring-fenced. S$30,000 total staged product exposure. Personal runway must be assessed separately. Small samples are decision signals, not proof of market-wide retention.

DAYS 15–30
Deliver five paid pilots
Measure current-method versus client-ready time. Track repeats. If the gate fails, change the job or stop; do not spend Gate 2.
DAYS 31–60
Build and verify the production workflow
Auth, catalogue, source-linked review, versioned export, billing and quotas. Test failed payments, duplicate webhooks, tenant isolation, deletion and backup restore before live customer data.
DAYS 61–90
Observe first monthly renewals
Invite ten subscribers, measure support and usage. Eight of ten renew once before growth spend. Day 90 is a decision, not a promised MRR milestone.


06 / Implementation contract
One application. Explicit authority.
Architecture
Commercial host + custom domain → same-origin TypeScript app/API → managed auth/PostgreSQL → private export storage. Server-only model adapter and Stripe Checkout/customer portal.
Agency-scoped records. Integer money values. Source references. Immutable export versions. Transactional usage reservations. Raw-body Stripe signature checks and idempotent order fulfillment.
No multi-agent system, vector database or Kubernetes. Model cannot execute tools, send emails or change catalogue rates.
Real launch prerequisites
  • Appropriate business registration, Stripe verification and bank settlement
  • Actual host/domain, price IDs, secrets and support contact
  • Terms, refunds, privacy, DPO/contact and processor contracts
  • Payment lifecycle, quotas, cross-tenant security and restore proof
  • Current tax/PDPA/marketing review; no unsupported compliance claim
Payment entitlement and failure states
A success page polls server state; it never grants access. Use verified paid invoices/current subscriptions for paid-through. Deduplicate webhook events, reconcile out-of-order changes, and handle failures, cancellation, refunds and disputes. Failed renewal: disclosed three-day grace, then no new generation; 30-day export window. Details and API contracts in the full blueprint.
Data handling policy to implement
Redacted input for pilots. Proposed defaults: source notes deleted 30 days after final export; proposals until deletion or 90 days after cancellation; active deletion within seven days; backups expire within 30 days. Separate statutory accounting retention. These are planned production policies, not capabilities of this local page. Verify overseas processing contracts and provider training/data terms before customer input.
What would invalidate this idea?
Owners do not pay, proposals are too infrequent, ChatGPT with a template works just as well, review takes more time than it saves, support destroys contribution, or paid cohorts do not renew. Interviews, paid pilots and actual workflow timings—not market-size slides—answer these questions.

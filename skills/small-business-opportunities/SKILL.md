# Small-Business Opportunities

Start with who needs something, why they need it now, and who can pay. Prefer a direct reply to an existing request when the seller does not want to post publicly. A Stripe link is a payment method, not a source of buyers. Do not turn a market trend into a claim that someone will buy.

## 1. Set the constraints

Reuse known location, skills, ready-to-sell assets, time budget, maximum spend, and cash-in-bank deadline. Default to no upfront spend for an urgent cash goal. Separate `cash by [date]` from `business to validate`; do not rank an attractive long-term model ahead of an order that can actually settle by the deadline. Ask only for unknowns that would change the next action.

## 2. Find buyers before designing the offer

- Search for recent request wording in marketplaces, local groups, business forums, and public procurement or job boards: `looking for`, `need someone to`, `recommend a`, `budget`, and the job to be done. Try adjacent wording and check the original thread, not just a search snippet. An expired or awarded tender, a listing with unclear status, or a request inaccessible to the seller is not an actionable lead.
- For each candidate record the source URL, post date, status as observed, buyer type, need, trigger/deadline, stated budget (or `unknown`), and a permitted reply path. Label evidence: **explicit buyer request**, **observed spend** (paid transaction or awarded contract), or **proxy** (ad, search interest, listed price, discussion). A competitor's asking price is not a paid price; an ad is not proof of profitable acquisition.
- Look at what buyers already do: spreadsheet, service provider, competitor product, or doing nothing. Check available alternatives and their price or effort. Do not invent leads, sales, conversion rates, or contact information. If no accessible open request matches, say so and give the next search or interview that would test the gap.

## 3. Choose the model that fits the evidence

| Model | Who pays and for what | Smallest useful validation |
|---|---|---|
| Custom report or fixed-scope service | A named buyer pays once to make a decision or solve a task | Reply to an open request with a defined deliverable, turnaround, and price. Show a small sample, not the full work. For a report, name the decision, coverage, cut-off date, and sources; use public, licensed, or buyer-authorized data. |
| Repeatable report or subscription | Multiple buyers pay for the same report, or for updates tied to a recurring decision | Sell the first version manually. Test whether buyers pay for a second edition before promising a subscription. |
| Advertising or lead generation | A client pays for an audit or campaign work; an advertiser may pay for placement or qualified leads | Without an existing audience, test a paid audit or client-approved pilot against a real buyer request before modeling sponsorship. Define qualified lead, attribution, and what is included in fees versus ad spend. No guarantee of sales; check consent and platform rules. |
| Dropshipping or niche e-commerce | A shopper buys a specific item delivered at a price and date they accept | Confirm a demand signal for the specific product, a reputable supplier, delivered quote, availability, return path, and realistic delivery window. Validate interest and inspect a sample before taking orders; do not list unverified stock, buy inventory, or run ads on proxy evidence alone. |

A buyer request for a one-off spreadsheet fix is not by itself evidence for a SaaS product. Prefer the simplest model that can serve the buyer now. Check legal, employer, data, product, and platform constraints that apply; don't accept work that requires confidential employer material or bypass marketplace payment rules.

## 4. Model one sale and the cash timeline

For each shortlisted opportunity provide **buyer → problem/trigger → offer → channel → price → delivery → reason to buy instead of the current alternative**. State whether revenue is one-time, recurring, sponsorship, or per qualified lead; do not assume repeats.

Separate observed inputs from assumptions. Use a calculator for:

- **Contribution per sale** = realized revenue net of taxes collected for remittance − product/supplier cost − shipping/duties borne by the seller − platform/payment fees − expected refund/return losses − delivery labor valued at the seller's hourly floor − acquisition cost. Count each cost once. If acquisition is ad-funded, estimate CAC as spend ÷ attributable paying customers, not clicks, and show the break-even CAC before spending. With zero purchases, CAC is not estimable.
- **Cash by deadline** = money expected in the bank by the deadline − cash outlays due by then. Account separately for supplier payments, ad spend, platform holds, and payout dates. A profitable order can still miss a bill.

Show a base and downside case when meaningful. Mark missing costs or conversion rates `unknown`; do not issue a precise profit or payout forecast from unverified inputs. Set aside opportunities with inaccessible buyers, negative plausible margins, spend above budget, or payout expected after a hard deadline.

## 5. Give the seller a decision and a test

Return up to three options, with evidence and direct links where available. Label each **pursue now**, **validate**, or **skip**, with the reason. For the strongest option, give a ready-to-send reply, the first permitted action, an effort/spend cap, and a dated pass/stop rule. Example validation policy (not a forecast): contact up to three matching open requests over 48 hours with zero ad spend; one buyer paying for a scoped pilot passes the first-sale test. Replies are interest, not revenue. If no qualified reply arrives, change the buyer segment or offer before spending.

For a direct order with an agreed scope and price, hand off to [Stripe Cashier](../stripe-cashier/SKILL.md) to verify checkout, payout timing, and successful payment before delivery. Do not send messages, publish listings, place orders, or spend money without explicit approval.

**Singapore fast-cash example:** If a reachable local buyer has asked for a CSV fix by tomorrow, model a one-time S$35 fixed-scope job with a redacted sample and a clearly defined output. Check the buyer's willingness to pay and the actual payout date. If this is a first Stripe payout and it misses the deadline, consider direct PayNow for a permitted direct sale. A supplier product with only search-interest evidence and unverified shipping belongs in `validate`, not `pursue now`.
